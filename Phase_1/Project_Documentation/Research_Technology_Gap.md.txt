# Research & Technology Gap

## 1. Introduction

Driver behaviour monitoring is an important area of Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS), with the objective of identifying unsafe driver behaviours before they lead to accidents.

The Phase 1 study identified several challenges in existing driver monitoring systems, including limited dataset diversity, poor generalization across different drivers and environments, false-positive alerts, computational limitations, and difficulties in understanding behaviour over time.

The research and technology gap analysis was performed to identify these limitations and determine the technical direction for the proposed project.

The initial Phase 1 investigation considered deep learning approaches such as CNNs, LSTM networks, and Autoencoders for driver behaviour anomaly detection. Based on further study and project development, the technical direction was refined toward a modular computer-vision and temporal-analysis architecture.

---

# 2. Research Gaps

## 2.1 Limited Dataset Diversity

Many existing driver behaviour detection systems are developed using datasets with limited variations in:

- Driver appearance
- Lighting conditions
- Camera positions
- Driver behaviours
- Facial characteristics
- Driving environments
- Occlusions

A model trained on a limited set of drivers or environments may not generalize effectively to unseen drivers and real-world conditions.

### Identified Gap

There is a need for more diverse driver behaviour data containing different people, behaviours, lighting conditions, and environmental situations.

### Project Direction

The project considers publicly available datasets together with a custom self-collected driver behaviour dataset to provide additional behavioural variation.

---

## 2.2 Limited Generalization

A driver monitoring model that performs well on its training dataset may not provide the same performance when applied to different drivers or environments.

Factors such as:

- Different facial structures
- Different seating positions
- Different camera angles
- Spectacles
- Lighting changes
- Partial face occlusion
- Different driving behaviours

can affect detection performance.

### Identified Gap

Existing systems may require improved robustness and generalization across different real-world conditions.

### Project Direction

The proposed system uses multiple complementary features, including facial landmarks, eye behaviour, mouth behaviour, head pose, and object information, instead of relying on only one visual feature.

---

## 2.3 Real-Time Computational Constraints

Real-time driver monitoring requires continuous processing of camera frames.

Using several computationally expensive deep learning models simultaneously can introduce:

- Processing delay
- Low FPS
- High CPU/GPU usage
- Increased system latency
- Reduced responsiveness

This is especially important when multiple modules such as face recognition, facial landmark detection, and object detection operate simultaneously.

### Identified Gap

There is a need for efficient architectures that can perform driver monitoring while maintaining acceptable real-time performance.

### Project Direction

The project follows a modular and optimized processing approach.

Different modules can operate at different processing frequencies instead of running every expensive model on every camera frame.
